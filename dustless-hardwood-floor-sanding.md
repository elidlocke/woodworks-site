A full sand and finish takes your floor back to bare wood and gives it a brand-new finish. It's the right choice for worn, scratched or greyed hardwood, and it's the only way to change the colour of a floor. I've been sanding floors in the Waterloo Region since 2004, and I do every job myself.

My sanders are connected to dust collection, so most of the dust is captured right at the machine instead of drifting through your house. No sanding is completely dust-free, but it's a world away from the old way of doing it. I'm a [Bona Certified Craftsman](/bona-certified-craftsman/), and I finish with Bona's water-based systems.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 1</p>
## The assessment {#assessment}
What I look for when I give you a quote
{: .step-sub}

Not every floor needs a full sand. When I look at your floor, in person or from your photos, here's what I'm checking:

- **Is the damage in the finish or in the wood?** If the scratches are only in the finish and the wood underneath is fine, a [buff and coat](/hardwood-floor-buff-and-coat/) may be all you need, and it costs much less. Grey or bare wood, deep scratches and dents mean a full sand.
- **How much wood is left.** Older floors may have been sanded several times already. I check how much wood is left above the tongue and groove, often by looking at the edge of a floor vent, so I know the floor can take another sanding safely.
- **Solid or engineered.** Engineered hardwood has a thin real-wood top layer. I measure it first, because sanding through it would ruin the floor.
- **Moisture and movement.** Cupped or crowned boards usually point to a moisture problem. That has to be found and fixed before sanding, or the boards will keep moving afterwards.
- **Stains and damaged boards.** Some stains sand out and some don't. I'll tell you honestly which ones will stay, and whether a [repair](/hardwood-floor-repair/) or a darker [stain](/hardwood-floor-staining/) is the better fix.
- **Size and extras.** The square footage, closets, stairs, carpet removal and repairs all go into the price.

If your floor doesn't need the work yet, I'll tell you.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 2</p>
## Getting ready {#getting-ready}
Prep to do before I start
{: .step-sub}

- **Empty the rooms.** All furniture, rugs and anything on the floor needs to be out of the rooms before I arrive, including the floors of any closets we're doing. My crew and I aren't movers and aren't insured to move furniture, so please arrange help or hire movers.
- **Take down fragile things.** Sanding machines vibrate. Take pictures, mirrors and anything breakable off the walls and shelves in the work area.
- **Pull up the carpet, or ask me to.** If there's carpet over the hardwood, I can remove it, pull the staples and tack strip, and set any raised nails so the floor is ready to sand. Let me know at the quote.
- **Plan how you'll live around it.** Think about how you'll get to the kitchen, bathroom and bedrooms while rooms are closed off. We can do the house in sections if it helps.
- **Pets and kids.** Keep them out of the work area for the whole job, including while the finish dries.
- **Choose your colour early.** If you'd like a stain, have a few colours in mind. I'll put samples on your actual floor once it's sanded.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 3</p>
## On the job {#on-the-job}
What happens while I'm there
{: .step-sub}

1. **Dustless sanding.** I start with a coarse grit and work up to a fine one, removing the old finish, wax and dirt while taking off as little wood as possible. My main machine is a [Lagler Hummel](/our-main-sanding-machine), chosen because it gives the flattest, most precise cut.
2. **Buffing.** An abrasive screen blends away the fine sanding marks so they're invisible under the finish.
3. **Staining, if you've chosen it.** I apply samples, you approve the colour, then I stain the floor.
4. **Finishing.** Two coats of water-based, low-VOC finish go on within about 36 hours. Between coats, the floor is screened and the edges are hand-sanded so each coat bonds properly.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 4</p>
## Aftercare {#aftercare}
Keeping your floor looking new
{: .step-sub}

- **Walk in socks after about 24 hours.** Wait until I give you the go-ahead, and keep shoes off for the first few days.
- **Bring furniture back gently.** Lift, don't drag, and put felt pads under every leg. I'll tell you when it's safe to move furniture back for your finish.
- **Keep rugs off for two to four weeks.** The finish keeps curing after it feels dry, and a rug laid too soon can mark it.
- **Clean the right way.** Sweep or dust mop often, because grit is what scratches a finish. To wash, use a damp (not wet) mop with a cleaner made for finished hardwood. Avoid steam mops, vinegar, oil soaps, waxes and "shine restorer" polishes. They can dull the finish and stop a future coat from bonding.
- **Use mats at the doors.** A mat inside and outside each entrance stops most of the grit before it reaches the floor.
- **Watch the humidity.** Hardwood is happiest at about 35–55% relative humidity. A humidifier in winter helps prevent gaps between the boards.
- **Refresh before it wears through.** When high-traffic areas start to look dull, a [buff and coat](/hardwood-floor-buff-and-coat/) puts a fresh coat on top and can keep you from needing another full sand for years.
