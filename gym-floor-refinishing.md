Gym floors take a beating, and they need to stay safe, consistent and good-looking for years. We refinish school gymnasiums and commercial hardwood floors across the Waterloo Region, including sanding, painting game lines and custom logos, and applying a durable finish. We've completed gym floors for local school boards, as well as retail spaces like the Artisan Zone store. You can see them on our [projects page](/hardwood-floor-refinishing-projects/).

<p class="step-kicker">Step 1</p>
## The assessment {#assessment}
What I look for when I give you a quote
{: .step-sub}

- **Does it need a full sand, or just a recoat?** If the finish is worn but the lines and the wood are fine, a screen and recoat is far quicker and cheaper. If the finish has worn through, the lines are faded or you want a new layout, it needs a full sand.
- **Game lines.** Which sports the floor is lined for (basketball, volleyball, badminton and so on), whether the current layout stays, and what colours you want.
- **Logos and artwork.** Size, number of colours and whether you have artwork ready all affect the time involved.
- **Condition of the floor.** I check for dead spots, gaps, damaged boards and signs of moisture, which need to be dealt with before finishing.
- **Schedule and access.** How long the gym can be closed, building access hours, and where equipment can be staged and parked.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 2</p>
## Getting ready {#getting-ready}
Prep to do before I start
{: .step-sub}

- **Book the closure.** Block off the gym for the whole job, including the curing time after the last coat. I'll give you the exact dates in the quote.
- **Clear the floor.** Remove all equipment, mats, nets, portable stages and anything stored around the edges. Retract bleachers wherever possible.
- **Confirm access.** Arrange keys or a contact for after-hours access, and let custodial staff know the schedule.
- **Keep heating and ventilation steady.** Steady temperature and humidity help the finish dry and cure properly. We'll go over what's needed at the quote.
- **Lock it down.** Make sure no one can walk in while the finish is wet or curing. One set of footprints means sanding and recoating that section.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 3</p>
## On the job {#on-the-job}
What happens while I'm there
{: .step-sub}

1. **Sanding.** The floor is sanded flat and clean with dust collection, through progressively finer grits.
2. **Sealing.** The bare wood is sealed and prepared for painting.
3. **Game lines and logos.** Lines are measured out, taped and painted, along with any custom logos.
4. **Finishing.** Coats of durable finish go on over the paint, with screening between coats for a strong bond.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 4</p>
## Aftercare {#aftercare}
Keeping your gym floor looking new
{: .step-sub}

- **Respect the cure time.** Keep the gym closed until the date I give you, even if the floor feels dry. Heavy use or equipment on a soft finish leaves marks.
- **Walk-off mats at every entrance.** Grit from outdoor shoes is the biggest cause of wear. Mats at the doors catch most of it.
- **Gym shoes only.** Keep street shoes, high heels and boots off the playing surface wherever possible.
- **Dust mop daily.** A daily dust mop keeps grit from grinding into the finish. Wash with a cleaner made for finished wood floors, and never flood the floor with water.
- **Protect it during events.** Use floor covers for assemblies, dances or anything with chairs and tables, and put protective feet on chairs and equipment.
- **Plan a regular recoat.** Busy gyms typically need a screen and recoat every year or so. Recoating on schedule keeps lines bright and can delay a full sand for years.
