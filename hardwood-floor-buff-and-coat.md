A buff and coat (also called a screen and recoat) puts a fresh layer of finish on a floor that's still in good shape. I lightly abrade the existing finish so the new coat can bond to it, then apply a new coat. Your floor gets its shine back, and the new coat protects the wood underneath.

It's the fastest and most affordable way to refresh hardwood, and it's usually done in a day. The trick is knowing when it will work and when it won't.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 1</p>
## The assessment {#assessment}
What I look for when I give you a quote
{: .step-sub}

A buff and coat only treats the finish, not the wood. So the first thing I'm checking is whether your problems are all in the finish:

- **Dull, hazy or lightly scratched finish?** Good candidate. Fine scratches that sit in the finish mostly disappear under a new coat.
- **Grey wood, bare spots or deep scratches?** Not a good candidate. Once the finish has worn through, the wood itself needs sanding. A new coat over grey wood just seals the grey in. You'll need a [full sand and finish](/dustless-hardwood-floor-sanding/).
- **Stains, or a colour change?** A buff and coat can't remove stains or change colour. Those need a full sand.
- **What's been used to clean it.** This one surprises people. Wax, oil soaps and "shine restorer" or polish products leave a residue that can stop a new coat from bonding. I'll ask what you've used, and check the floor before I recommend a buff and coat.
- **Factory prefinished floors.** Many prefinished floors have a very hard factory finish that new coats don't stick to easily. Some can be recoated with the right bonding process, and some aren't worth it. I'll tell you which yours is.

If a buff and coat won't give you a result you're happy with, I'll tell you before you spend the money.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 2</p>
## Before I arrive {#before-i-arrive}
How to get your home ready
{: .step-sub}

- **Stop using polishes now.** From the day you book, clean only with a damp mop and a cleaner made for finished hardwood. No wax, oil soap or shine products.
- **Empty the rooms.** All furniture and rugs need to be out of the rooms. My crew and I aren't movers and aren't insured to move furniture, so please arrange help.
- **Give it a sweep.** A quick sweep or vacuum helps. I'll do the detailed cleaning before the coat goes on.
- **Plan for a day.** Expect the rooms to be closed for the day of the work and while the coat dries. Keep pets and kids out of the area.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 3</p>
## On the job {#on-the-job}
What happens while I'm there
{: .step-sub}

1. **Buffing.** I go over the whole floor with a buffer and abrasive screen. This dulls the old finish and gives the new coat something to grip.
2. **Cleaning.** All the dust from buffing is removed, so nothing gets trapped in the new finish.
3. **Coating.** A fresh coat of finish goes on. I finish with Bona's water-based systems, which are low-odour and dry quickly.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 4</p>
## Aftercare {#aftercare}
Keeping your floor looking new
{: .step-sub}

- **Give it time before walking on it.** I'll tell you when you can walk on the floor in socks. Keep shoes off for the first few days.
- **Furniture back with felt pads.** Lift, don't drag. Put felt pads under every leg, and replace them when they wear down.
- **Keep rugs off for a couple of weeks.** The new coat keeps curing after it's dry to the touch.
- **Clean the same way you prepped.** Dust mop often, and wash with a damp (not wet) mop and a hardwood floor cleaner. Avoid steam mops, vinegar, oil soaps, waxes and polishes. They make the next buff and coat harder, too.
- **Book the next one early.** A buff and coat every few years, before the finish wears through, can put off a full sand for a long time.
