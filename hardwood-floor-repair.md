A damaged area doesn't always mean a whole new floor. Badly stained, cracked or water-damaged boards can be cut out and replaced. Then, when the floor is sanded and finished, the new boards are sanded and finished together with the old ones so they blend in.

Where I can, I use reclaimed hardwood, because older wood is the closest match to the colour and age of an existing floor. When reclaimed wood isn't available, I use new boards, which age to blend in over time.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 1</p>
## The assessment {#assessment}
What I look for when I give you a quote
{: .step-sub}

- **How deep the stain goes.** Grey marks are often near the surface and may sand out. Dark or black stains, from pet urine or long-standing water, usually go right into the wood. I'll tell you which boards will clean up and which need replacing.
- **Whether the cause is fixed.** There's no point replacing boards over a leak that's still dripping, or in a spot a pet still uses. The source has to be sorted first.
- **Moisture and the subfloor.** Cupped, soft or springy boards can mean the subfloor underneath is damaged too. I'll check how the floor feels and whether it has dried out.
- **Matching the wood.** Species, board width and age all matter. I'll look at what's needed to get a close match, and whether reclaimed boards are available.
- **Repair or stain?** Sometimes a darker [stain](/hardwood-floor-staining/) can blend minor marks well enough that replacement isn't needed. I'll give you both options if that's the case.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 2</p>
## Before I arrive {#before-i-arrive}
How to get your home ready
{: .step-sub}

- **Fix the source.** Get any leaks repaired (radiators, dishwashers, windows, ice makers) before the repair is booked.
- **Let it dry.** Water-damaged floors need to dry out fully before they're repaired or sanded. I'll tell you if the floor needs more time.
- **Keep pets away from the area.** If a pet has been using a spot, keep them off it until the repair is done.
- **Empty the rooms.** Furniture and rugs need to be out of the rooms where we're working, and out of any rooms we'll be refinishing at the same time.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 3</p>
## On the job {#on-the-job}
What happens while I'm there
{: .step-sub}

1. **Cutting out the damage.** The damaged boards are carefully removed without disturbing the boards around them.
2. **Fitting new boards.** Reclaimed or new boards are cut to fit, woven into the existing floor and fastened down.
3. **Sanding and finishing together.** Repairs are always done before sanding, so the old and new boards are sanded, stained if needed and finished as one floor. See [sand and finish](/dustless-hardwood-floor-sanding/) for how that part goes.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 4</p>
## Aftercare {#aftercare}
Preventing the next repair
{: .step-sub}

- **Wipe up spills and accidents right away.** Water and urine do the most damage when they sit. A finish buys you time, but not forever.
- **Mats under water bowls and plants.** A waterproof mat under pet bowls and plant pots prevents the most common water stains I see.
- **Keep an eye on appliances.** Check under dishwashers, fridges with ice makers and around radiators now and then for slow leaks.
- **Follow normal floor care.** Socks only for the first day, rugs off for two to four weeks, felt pads under furniture, and a damp mop with a hardwood cleaner, never a wet one.
- **New boards change colour.** New wood will darken a little as it ages, especially in sunny rooms. That's normal, and it helps it blend in more over time.
