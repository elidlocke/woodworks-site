Staining is an optional step in a [sand and finish](/dustless-hardwood-floor-sanding/), and it's the easiest way to completely change the look of a room. A dated orange oak floor can become a rich brown, a modern natural tone or something much darker. It's done after the floor is sanded and before the finish goes on, so it's part of the same job.

The colour on a sample card is never quite the colour you'll get, though. Every species, and every floor, takes stain differently. That's why I test on your floor before you choose.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 1</p>
## The assessment {#assessment}
What I look for when I give you a quote
{: .step-sub}

- **Your wood species.** Red oak takes stain evenly and predictably. White oak can go a little greyer or more muted. Maple and birch have a tight grain that can take stain blotchily, especially in darker colours, so I'll be upfront if your floor isn't suited to the colour you have in mind. Pine is soft and can look patchy too.
- **What you want it to match.** If you're matching other floors, trim or cabinets, I'll want to see them, and if new boards are going in, they need to be stained to blend in with the old. One of our Waterloo customers had new hardwood installed in a carpeted hallway, and we stained the whole upstairs so it all matched.
- **Stains you're hoping to hide.** A darker colour can help blend old water and pet marks that sanding doesn't fully remove. I'll tell you how much it will help before you count on it.
- **How the room is lit and used.** Very dark floors look striking but show dust, pet hair and scratches more. Very light floors hide them better. I'll help you weigh that up for how you live.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 2</p>
## Before I arrive {#before-i-arrive}
How to get your home ready
{: .step-sub}

- **Shortlist two or three colours.** Bring photos of floors you like, or tell me the look you're after. I'll suggest colours that suit your wood.
- **Plan to be home for the samples.** After sanding, I put sample patches on your actual floor. You'll need to look at them, ideally in daylight, and choose before I go ahead. Sometimes we mix a custom blend.
- **Think about the whole floor plan.** Decide which connected rooms, hallways and stairs should be the same colour.
- **The usual sanding prep.** Rooms empty of furniture and rugs, fragile items off the walls, pets and kids out of the work area. The [sand and finish page](/dustless-hardwood-floor-sanding/#before-i-arrive) has the full list.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 3</p>
## On the job {#on-the-job}
What happens while I'm there
{: .step-sub}

1. **Sanding.** The floor is sanded to bare wood. Even sanding matters more for a stained floor, because stain shows any unevenness.
2. **Samples.** I lay down sample patches and you choose your colour.
3. **Staining.** The stain is applied to the whole floor and left to dry fully.
4. **Finishing.** Three coats of clear finish go on top to protect the colour.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 4</p>
## Aftercare {#aftercare}
Keeping your floor looking new
{: .step-sub}

A stained floor is cared for just like any other refinished floor, with a few extra points:

- **Wait for the go-ahead.** Socks only after about 24 hours, and keep rugs off for two to four weeks while the finish cures.
- **Dust more often on dark floors.** Dark stains show dust and pet hair, so a quick dust mop every day or two keeps them looking their best.
- **Protect the finish to protect the colour.** The stain sits under the finish. If the finish wears through in walkways, the colour wears with it. Felt pads, door mats and keeping pet nails trimmed all help.
- **Clean gently.** Use a damp mop and a cleaner made for finished hardwood. Avoid steam mops, vinegar, oil soaps and polishes.
- **Recoat before it wears through.** A [buff and coat](/hardwood-floor-buff-and-coat/) adds a fresh layer of finish over the colour without sanding it away.
