Stairs take more wear than almost any other part of a house, and worn treads are one of the first things people notice when they walk in. I refinish hardwood stair treads, usually at the same time as the main floor so everything matches in colour and sheen.

Stairs are hand work. Each tread is sanded and finished individually, with extra care on the nosing (the rounded front edge) and the corners where the big sanders can't reach.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 1</p>
## The assessment {#assessment}
What I look for when I give you a quote
{: .step-sub}

- **Are the treads real hardwood?** Treads that have always been bare are usually hardwood. Treads that have been under carpet may be hardwood, or may be construction-grade wood that was never meant to be seen. I'll check before quoting.
- **Solid wood or veneer risers?** If your risers (the vertical part of each step) are original solid wood, I can refinish them too. Most risers are veneer, though, and I don't sand veneer, because it's too easy to sand through it. Many people paint veneer risers instead, which looks great with wood treads.
- **Carpet damage.** Carpeted stairs leave staple and tack holes. Most can be filled, but I'll tell you what to expect.
- **Spindles and railings.** I work around them. If you're planning to replace them, have a finish carpenter do that first.
- **How many steps, and what shape.** Straight runs, landings, winders and open-sided stairs all take different amounts of time, so they're priced differently.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 2</p>
## Before I arrive {#before-i-arrive}
How to get your home ready
{: .step-sub}

- **Plan another way up and down.** This is the big one. If the stairs are the only way to the bedrooms, we'll plan the timing with you. That might mean doing the stairs in two stages, or staying somewhere else for a night.
- **Clear the stairs and landings.** Take everything off the steps, landings and any walls you might brush against.
- **Carpet off, or ask me.** If the stairs are carpeted, let me know whether you'll remove it or you'd like me to. All the staples and tack strip need to come out before sanding.
- **Pets and kids.** Gate off the stairs for the whole job, including while the finish dries.
- **Painting risers?** If you'll be painting the risers, talk to me about timing, so paint and finish don't end up on each other.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 3</p>
## On the job {#on-the-job}
What happens while I'm there
{: .step-sub}

1. **Sanding.** Each tread is sanded by hand with smaller sanders and scrapers, through the same grits as a floor.
2. **Staining, if you've chosen it.** Stairs are stained to match the floor, so the colour flows from one to the other.
3. **Finishing.** The treads get the same coats of finish as the floors, with each coat lightly screened so the next one bonds.

<p class="step-kicker">Step 4</p>
## Aftercare {#aftercare}
Keeping your stairs looking new
{: .step-sub}

- **Stay off until I say so.** Then go up and down carefully. Freshly finished treads can be slippery in socks, so take the railing.
- **Keep runners off for two to four weeks.** If you're adding a runner or stair treads, wait until the finish has fully cured.
- **Wipe, don't soak.** Vacuum or wipe the treads, and wash with a damp cloth and a cleaner made for finished hardwood. Never use oil soaps or polishes on stairs. They can make the treads slippery and stop a future coat from bonding.
- **Mind the nosing.** The front edge of each tread takes the most wear. Keep pet nails trimmed, and refresh the finish before it wears through to bare wood.
